## Understanding Self-Soothing

Self-soothing is often misunderstood as "cry it out" parenting, but this article clarifies that the practice involves "allowing babies to learn calming strategies" to develop "an important life skill."

## Core Principles

The article emphasizes that teaching self-soothing requires parents to first determine what their baby genuinely needs. Before attempting calming techniques, caregivers should verify the baby is "fed, changed, and well rested."

Parents are advised to "slow down and take steps to really learn about your baby" rather than immediately picking them up when crying occurs. The approach recommends trying "each technique slowly, and pause to see how your baby responds."

## The CALM Baby Method

The article presents a 10-step progression, designed so "you are doing less at the beginning by just using your voice and more at the end when you are holding." The sequence moves from:

1. Eye contact
2. Talking
3. Hand placement
4. Positioning adjustments
5. Side rolling
6. Picking up
7. Rocking
8. Swaddling
9. Pacifier use
10. Feeding

## Additional Strategies

Complementary techniques include massage, singing, walking, and white noise. The article cautions against overstimulation, noting that "sometimes this means we are adding more stimulation to an already overwhelmed sensory system."

## Timing and Consistency

Parents should "try one strategy (1 or 2 sensory inputs) for about 5 minutes before moving on." Consistency matters—"your baby may be getting used to it and may calm more quickly over time."

As babies grow, parents should adapt strategies, remaining "open to when it is time to try something different that may be more suitable or safer."

## Professional Support

The article recommends consulting pediatricians or specialists like pediatric occupational therapists or infant mental health specialists if frustration persists.
