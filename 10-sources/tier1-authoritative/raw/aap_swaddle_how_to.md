## Overview

The article explains that babies experience the Moro (startle) reflex, which causes them to "jerk suddenly, flail their arms and legs, and even cry out in response to being startled." Swaddling, or the "burrito wrap," helps prevent self-startling during sleep.

## Swaddling in 6 Steps

1. Lay a thin baby blanket out like a diamond in front of you.
2. Fold the top corner of the blanket down a bit so that the folded corner almost reaches the middle of the blanket.
3. Place your infant on their back and center them on the blanket with their arms at their sides, their head just above the folded edge and their shoulders just below it.
4. Take one of the side corners of the blanket and fold it over your baby's shoulder and across his body, making sure to tuck the corner underneath him on the opposite side.
5. Then take the bottom corner of the blanket (below your baby's feet) and fold it up over your baby. If the blanket is large enough that the bottom corner reaches up to (or over) your baby's face, you can simply fold it back down until their face is no longer covered or bring it over one or the other shoulder and tuck it under them.
6. Finally, take the only remaining corner and pull it over your baby's other shoulder and across their body. Again, tuck this corner snugly under your baby's opposite side.

## Safe Swaddling Guidelines

**Hip and Joint Development:** "A baby's hips should not be bundled too tightly. Instead, they should be allowed to relax in their natural frog-leg positions to allow for proper growth and joint development."

**Sleep Position:** Always place swaddled babies on their back—never on side or stomach.

**Overheating Risk:** Monitor for "sweating, damp hair, rash and rapid breathing."

**When to Stop:** Discontinue swaddling once your baby attempts to roll over.

**Breathing Safety:** You should "be able to fit two or three fingers between the swaddle and your baby's chest."

**Weighted Objects:** "The American Academy of Pediatrics does not recommend using weighted swaddles, weighted blankets or other weighted objects."
