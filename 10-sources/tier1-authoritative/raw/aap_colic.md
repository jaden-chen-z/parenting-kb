## Definition and Prevalence

Colic affects "about one-fifth of all babies" and typically emerges "between the second and fourth weeks." The condition is characterized by inconsolable crying where infants often scream, extend or pull up their legs, and pass gas.

## Duration and Timeline

Regular fussiness peaks at approximately 3 hours daily by 6 weeks, then declines to 1-2 hours by 3-4 months. Colicky crying "stops by 3 to 4 months, but it can last until 6 months of age."

## Causes

The article explains that "colic means simply that the child is unusually sensitive to stimulation" and may struggle with self-soothing due to an immature nervous system. In breastfeeding infants, it can signal food sensitivity in the parent's diet. Less commonly, colic may indicate medical issues like hernias.

## Treatment Recommendations

**Dietary approaches:**
- Breastfeeding parents can eliminate milk products, caffeine, onions, cabbage—one at a time, observing results over 2 weeks
- Formula-fed infants may benefit from protein hydrolysate formula in rare cases

**Comfort techniques:**
- Use baby carriers for motion and contact
- Try rhythmic sounds (vacuum, dryer, white-noise machines)
- Introduce pacifiers
- Practice swaddling
- Gentle back rubs with baby positioned tummy-down across your knees

**Parental self-care:** Seek breaks and support; never shake an infant.

## When to Consult a Pediatrician

Parents should "consult your pediatrician to make sure that the crying is not related to any serious medical condition."
