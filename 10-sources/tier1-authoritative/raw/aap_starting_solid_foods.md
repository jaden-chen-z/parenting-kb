## When Can My Baby Begin Solid Foods?

Readiness depends on individual development. Consider these readiness signs:

- **Head control**: Baby should sit in a high chair with good head control
- **Mouth opening**: Baby watches you eat, reaches for food, seems eager to be fed
- **Swallowing ability**: Baby can move food from spoon to throat (pushing it out initially is normal)
- **Size**: "Generally, when infants double their birth weight (typically at about 4 months of age) and weigh about 13 pounds or more, they may be ready"

## AAP Breastfeeding Recommendation

The AAP "recommends providing your baby only breast milk for approximately 6 months after birth." After 6 months, introduce foods while continuing breastfeeding for "2 years or beyond."

## How to Feed Your Baby

Start with minimal amounts—"half a spoonful or less"—and gradually increase to "just a teaspoonful or two to start." Expect most food to end up on the baby's face and clothing initially. If your baby refuses, pause and try again later rather than forcing feeding.

## First Foods Selection

Choose from any food group with these guidelines:

- Foods must be "soft or pureed to prevent choking"
- "Introduce one 'single-ingredient' new food from any food group every 3 to 5 days"
- Include iron and zinc sources like meat or "iron-fortified cereals"
- "There is no evidence that waiting to introduce baby-safe (soft) foods, such as eggs, dairy, soy, peanut products or fish, beyond 4 to 6 months of age prevents food allergy"
- "There is no evidence that your baby will develop a dislike for vegetables if fruit is given first"

## Bottle Cereal Warning

"Do not put baby cereal in a bottle because your baby could choke. It may also increase the amount of food your baby eats and can cause your baby to gain too much weight."

## Choking Hazards (Under 1 Year)

Avoid: hot dogs, nuts, seeds, meat chunks, cheese chunks, whole grapes, popcorn, peanut butter chunks, raw vegetables, fruit chunks, and hard/sticky candy.

## Finger Foods

Once baby can "sit up and bring their hands or other objects to their mouth," offer soft finger foods like "small pieces of banana, wafer-type cookies or crackers; scrambled eggs; well-cooked pasta."

## Juice and Water

- Babies under 12 months: "should not be given juice"
- Ages 12-36 months: "give only 100% fruit juice and no more than 4 ounces a day"
- Water: Healthy babies don't need extra water initially; limit to "no more than 1 cup (8 ounces) each day" when introduced

## Expected Changes

Stools become "more solid and variable in color" with "much stronger odor." Green vegetables may cause deep-green stools; beets may turn them red. Undigested food pieces are normal. Loose, watery stools with mucus may indicate digestive irritation.
