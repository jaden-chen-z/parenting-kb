# Suitable Sleeping Sites: Safe at Night & Naptime

## Crib Safety

**Crib Slats**
The slats should be no more than 2-3/8 inches apart. All new cribs must meet this standard, but older cribs may not. Avoid using any crib that does not meet this 2011 standard.

**Posts & Cutouts**
Steer clear of bedposts taller than 1/16th of an inch and/or cutouts in the headboard or any other parts of the crib, where a baby's or toddler's body parts could get stuck.

**Bumpers and Pillows**
For safety's sake, keep crib bumpers and pillows out of your baby's crib.

**Crib Toys**
Keep all stuffed animals (and most toys) out of your newborn's crib because they can pose a small but nevertheless real safety risk. The exceptions are the types of toys that strap securely to the side of the crib.

**Mobiles**
If you do choose to use mobiles, make sure they do not hang low enough to entangle your baby, especially once they begin to roll. Once your baby is able to sit up, it will definitely be time for their mobile to come down.

**Crib Placement**
Place your crib well away from any windows and no less than an arm's reach away from any nearby dressers or tabletops. Limit your over-the-crib wall decorations to painted walls and wallpaper. Picture frames and mirrors over cribs may be cute, but they are also injuries waiting to happen.

**Firm-Fitting Mattress/Fitted Sheet**
Make sure you end up with a mattress that fits snugly into your chosen crib. Any extra space between the mattress and crib frame has the potential to trap a baby's arm, leg or head. Also make sure your fitted sheets are tight enough that they don't slip off easily.

**Tooth-Resistant Rails**
Some railings are covered by a special plastic to prevent teething babies from gnawing on the paint or wood.

**Adjustable Mattress Height**
Many cribs have adjustable heights so you can lower the mattress as your baby gets taller. Keep it at the highest level while your newborn is relatively immobile. By the time your baby is able to sit or stand up, lower the level of the crib mattress accordingly.

## Bare is Best When it Comes to Cribs

The AAP recommends that nothing but a snugly fitted sheet be placed with your baby in the crib during the first year.

## Why Drop-Side Cribs are a Thing of the Past

In years past, crib railings were almost always adjustable. Numerous injuries from crib side-rails resulted in the largest crib recall in history (2.1 million cribs!) in 2009. As a result, the Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC), which sets voluntary industry safety standards, required that going forward, all full-sized cribs be manufactured with 4 immovable sides. Always check out the latest safety information on the CPSC website before dropping your guard.

## Bed-Sharing Safety Concerns

Recent studies suggest that bed-sharing may significantly increase the risk of infant suffocation. Many experts (including those responsible for writing AAP policy) now strongly advise against it and instead suggest the very practical and safer alternative of sharing the same room but not the same bed during your baby's first year.

If there is a chance that you may fall asleep with your baby in your bed, here are some extremely important safety considerations:

**Make Your Bed Like a Crib**
The heavy blankets, comforters, pillows, and other accessories typically found on adult beds can suffocate or smother a baby and therefore have no place being in the same location where newborns sleep.

**Bed-Sharing Babies are at Risk from Falls**
Bed-sharing babies are at risk from falls or the possibility of being trapped between the mattress and the wall, headboard, or other furniture.

**Bed-Sharing and the Use of Tobacco, Alcohol, or Drugs Don't Mix**
Tobacco, alcohol, or drugs, including over-the-counter or prescription medications, all have the distinct potential to cause excessive drowsiness or impaired judgment, making those who choose to indulge at risk of being less aware of a baby in the bed.
