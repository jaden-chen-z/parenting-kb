The most obvious difference between bottle-feeding and breastfeeding is that "the bottle lets you see how much your baby is drinking." Each baby has different needs, and "babies know how much food they need to grow" when parents respond to hunger cues.

## Choosing A Bottle

Manufacturers market various nipple and bottle designs, but scientific evidence doesn't clearly support one brand over others.

## How Many Ounces?

Newborns typically consume:
- First 2-3 days: 1-2 ounces (30-60 mL) per feeding
- After initial days: 2-3 ounces (60-90 mL) every 3-4 hours
- First month onward: approximately 4 ounces every 4 hours
- By 6 months: 6-8 ounces (180-240 mL), 4-5 times daily

A general guideline: babies consume "around 2½ ounces (75 mL) per day" for every pound of body weight. Growth assessment occurs at wellness examinations.

## Hunger Cues

Babies signal hunger by rooting, scooting, sucking their hand, and "smacking her lips." They indicate fullness by releasing the nipple, turning away, falling asleep, or—as they grow—"look up and grin at you."

## Feeding FAQs

Common concerns addressed:
- Overfeeding is rare; babies typically need no more than "7 or 8 ounces per feeding or more than 36 ounces in a day"
- Babies may suck for comfort rather than hunger
- Most babies sleep through the night between 2-4 months or at approximately 12+ pounds

## What You Can Do

**Positioning**: Support baby in a "semi-upright position, with her head cradled in the crook of your arm." Hold the bottle so "milk completely covers the nipple." Avoid feeding while baby lies flat, as this increases choking risk and can cause middle ear infections.

## When to Call the Doctor

"If your baby is taking more than 7 or 8 ounces per feeding or more than 36 ounces a day, address it with her doctor." Overfeeding contributes to spitting up and obesity.
