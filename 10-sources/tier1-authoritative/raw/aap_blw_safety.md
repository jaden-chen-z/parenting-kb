## Definition & Basics

Baby-led weaning (BLW), or "auto-weaning," transitions infants from breast milk or formula to solid foods. The AAP notes babies are "usually ready to try their first few bites of solid food" around 6 months, sometimes as early as 4 months.

## How BLW Works

The method involves offering "small portions of whatever's on the family menu" with pieces sized for grasping but small enough to prevent choking. Babies self-feed at their own pace while sitting with family, and parents "don't require them to finish all their food." Through daily practice, infants learn appropriate portion sizes through natural gagging and spitting out excess.

## Potential Benefits

The article identifies possible advantages:
- Fostering independence through self-feeding
- Exposure to "more flavors and textures," potentially reducing picky eating
- Emphasis on whole foods rather than processed purees
- Supporting satiety cues to prevent obesity
- Developing motor skills through grasping and chewing

## Safety Concerns

Key risks include:

**Nutritional gaps**: Babies "may take in too few calories" if preferring fruits over nutrient-dense foods like meats and cheeses. Self-selected diets may lack sufficient iron and micronutrients.

**Choking risk**: The article states "choking is one of the leading causes of unintentional death for infants." However, it notes "some studies suggest baby-led weaning does not pose higher choking risks than traditional feeding."

## AAP Recommendations

The Academy emphasizes flexibility: families can combine BLW with spoon-feeding without guilt, as "pureed foods can be nutritious, too." Parents should introduce allergen-rich foods like "peanut butter, dairy and eggs around 6 months" to potentially prevent allergies, which may require deviating from strict BLW protocols.

## Safety Guidelines

- Use high chairs with secured harnesses
- Cut foods into "finger-shaped pieces (similar to a small baby carrot)"
- Avoid "round, coin-shaped slices" that increase choking risk
- Prioritize soft foods: ripe bananas, steamed vegetables, shredded meats, eggs, yogurt
- Monitor for gagging and choking; know emergency clearance techniques
- Consult pediatricians before starting BLW

## Readiness Considerations

Not all babies are developmentally ready at 6 months. Those "born prematurely, has difficulty sitting up on their own, or struggles to chew and swallow even soft solids" should wait. The article emphasizes watching for "signs of readiness without rushing."

## Nutritional Support

"Breast milk or formula help fill nutritional gaps" while babies explore new foods, making continued supplementation essential alongside solid food introduction.
