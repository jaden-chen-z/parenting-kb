## Ready or Not

A baby's digestive system isn't prepared to process cereal until approximately 6 months of age. "When he is old enough to digest cereal, he should also be ready to eat it from a spoon." The AAP recommends breastfeeding as the sole nutrition source for about 6 months, then continuing alongside solid foods until at least 12 months.

## Too Hard to Handle

Introducing cereal before developmental readiness increases choking risks. "Offering cereal in a bottle (or even on a spoon) before babies are developmentally ready can increase the likelihood of gagging and/or inhaling the thickened mixture into their lungs."

## Allergy Activation

Early solid food exposure before 4 months may trigger future food allergies. Delaying introduction until 4-6 months minimizes this risk.

## Overfeeding

The primary concern involves unintended caloric overconsumption. Infants naturally regulate milk intake by volume, but adding cereal obscures this mechanism. "Putting cereal in the bottle is considered by some to be a form of force-feeding that can cause babies to 'overdose' on calories."
