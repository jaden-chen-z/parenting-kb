Healthy, growing babies usually do not need to be awakened to breastfeed or take a bottle.

## When to Check with Your Pediatrician

Consult your pediatrician about nighttime awakening if your baby is not:

- Growing and gaining weight steadily
- Feeding well 8 to 12 times daily (breastfeeding) or 5 to 8 times daily (bottle-fed or older infant)
- Urinating normally with at least 4 wet diapers per day
- Having at least 3 normal bowel movements daily (breastfeeding babies typically have more frequent, soft, seedy movements)

## Concrete Ways to Help Newborns Learn How to Sleep

- Help baby fall asleep through soothing techniques like rocking, thumb sucking, or nonnutritive breast suckling
- Never place baby in crib with a bottle—sugary liquids promote tooth decay and ear infections
- Provide lots of attention while baby is awake to help baby feel calm and secure
- Pay attention to sleepiness and overtiredness signs to help baby fall asleep before becoming overtired

## What It Really Means to be a Good Sleeper

"A good sleeper is a child who wakes up frequently but can get himself back to sleep. It is not a child who sleeps without waking for 10 hours at night." Frequent waking is developmentally appropriate and allows babies to wake when not getting enough oxygen or experiencing breathing problems. Prolonged undisturbed sleep at this age is not healthy.
